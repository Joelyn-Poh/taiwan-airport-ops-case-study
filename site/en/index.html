Taoyuan Airport ride-hailing reward analysis

Turning messy ride data into a decision we can act on

Do rewards really create more rides, and are those extra rides worth the cost?

One-sentence conclusion The campaign worked,
but do not scale it yet.

Trips increased, but reward cost, budget control, and airport wait time all missed the operating standard.

  1. 01AskIs the reward worth it?
  2. 02CleanRemove invalid records first
  3. 03CompareUse Control as the baseline
  4. 04DecideCheck effect, cost, and service

This project uses synthetic data. It does not represent a real ride-hailing platform and does not use any company's internal data.

01

Project idea

Why did I choose this topic?

I placed myself in the role of an operations analyst at a ride-hailing platform. The simulated business partners with a travel company and sends ride rewards to travelers arriving at Taoyuan Airport.

A basic campaign report might only ask whether orders increased. That is not enough. If more orders also create losses, overspending, or longer passenger waits, the platform should not scale the campaign.

Question ADid it work?

Did the reward groups complete more airport rides than the no-reward Control group?

Question BDid it pay back?

Was the added contribution enough to cover the reward cost?

Question CCould operations handle it?

Did driver supply, pickup time, and cancellation remain within acceptable limits?

Scenario

A travel partner supplies confirmed arrival bookings. Before arrival, the platform sends a CRM message and then observes ride behavior for seven days.

Five success conditions

  1. 01

    Airport completion is higher than Control.

  2. 02

    Added contribution is higher than reward cost.

  3. 03

    Taoyuan Airport P90 pickup time rises by no more than 10%.

  4. 04

  5. 05

What is P90 pickup time? In plain language, about 90 out of 100 rides are picked up within this time. It shows long waits better than a simple average.

03

Data design

Why did I deliberately add bad records?

Real ride data comes from passenger apps, driver systems, payments, CRM, and travel partners. Different systems may send duplicates, arrive late, or omit required fields.

A perfectly clean dataset would hide the most important preparation work. I therefore mixed realistic errors into synthetic data and used SQL to find, classify, and handle them.

Invalid data added on purpose

  • Duplicate ride orders and events
  • Unreadable timestamps or future events
  • Completion times earlier than pickup times
  • Missing, 0,0, or out-of-Taiwan coordinates
  • Orders with no matching passenger or driver
  • Negative fares or payments greater than fares
  • A reward user different from the ride user
  • Reward redemption in the Control group
  • Events arriving more than one day late
04

Analysis process

How did I move from raw data to a decision?

  1. Step 01

    Keep the raw data

    Load every CSV into raw_* tables without changing the source records.

  2. Step 02

    Standardize formats

    Trim spaces, normalize status values, and safely convert time, coordinates, and amounts.

  3. Step 03

    Handle duplicates

    Keep the earliest receipt for each trip_id and flag later copies.

  4. Step 04

    Check business logic

    Validate time order, coordinates, users, drivers, fares, payments, and reward eligibility.

  5. Step 05

    Rebuild each ride

    Use events to reconstruct requested → accepted → picked_up → completed.

  6. Step 06

    Select the campaign cohort

    Keep confirmed arrivals with CRM consent and no completed Taiwan trip in the previous 180 days.

  7. Step 07

    Compare the three groups

    Measure message engagement, airport completion, D7 repeat, reward cost, and added contribution.

  8. Step 08

    Check operating health

    Review acceptance, cancellation, pickup time, driver supply, and daily budget by zone and hour.

06

Recommendation

Do not scale yet. Redesign the offer first.

Final judgmentEffective
does not mean scalable
  1. Both reward designs had negative added contribution.
  2. Campaign spend reached 139.3% of budget.
  3. Airport pickup time exceeded the operating limit.

What would I change?

  1. 01
    Keep the Control group

    Measure natural ride behavior and avoid crediting the campaign for rides that would happen anyway.

  2. 02
    Redesign Bundle first

    Lower the second reward, require a minimum fare, or share the cost with the travel partner.

  3. 03
    Pace CRM sends

    Match message volume to hourly airport driver supply instead of releasing all demand at once.

  4. 04
    Add automatic stop rules

    Pause new messages when pickup time, cancellation, or budget crosses its limit.

Project limitations

  • All records are synthetic and cannot represent a real platform's results.
  • Weather, flight delay, competing transport, and competitor prices are not included.
  • Real partner cost and customer support cases are not available.
  • The eight-week window cannot measure long-term retention.

What I learned

What did this project teach me?

Data analysis does not end when numbers enter a chart. First, the data must be trustworthy. Then campaign growth must be checked together with cost, budget, and service quality.

SQL was not only a calculation tool. It documented each rule so another person could follow the same tables and commands and challenge the decision.

More rides are useful only when the business can afford and serve them.
